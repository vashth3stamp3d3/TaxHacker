# T4002 Self-employed Business, Professional, Commission, Farming, and Fishing Income

Official source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html

CRA guide version observed: 2025 online/PDF guide available as t4002-25e.pdf. Page details observed: 2026-04-10.

## Current versus capital expenses

T4002 is primarily for self-employed business, professional, commission, farming, and fishing income. For a corporation, it is not the T2 filing guide, but it is useful general CRA context for business income and expense concepts.

The guide discusses business expense classification, including:

- Current expenses: recurring expenses or costs that restore property to original condition.
- Capital expenses: costs that provide lasting benefit, improve property beyond original condition, represent a separate asset, or are significant relative to the property.
- Capital cost allowance context for capital property.

A print shop typically expenses paper, ink, toner, production supplies, and short-lived shop consumables as current costs. Presses, wide-format printers, finishing equipment, and major leasehold improvements are usually capital and recovered through capital cost allowance rather than an immediate deduction. Software subscriptions are often current; perpetual software licences and hardware may be capital. Mixed invoices should be split.

## Owner-paid and mixed-use costs

When an owner pays a business bill personally, the corporation may still have a deductible business expense if the cost is incurred to earn income, but the other side of the entry is usually a shareholder loan rather than a personal drawing. Personal living expenses, fines, and most club dues are not deductible. Meals and entertainment are often limited. Home-office and vehicle costs for a corporation follow different rules than for a sole proprietor; do not copy T4002 percentages onto a T2 without checking T4012 and the relevant schedules.

Keep invoices, GST numbers for ITC claims, and a business purpose. GST/HST ITCs are a sales-tax concept from RC4022, not an income-tax deduction from T4002.

## Advisor usage

Use T4002 cautiously for general business expense reasoning, especially current vs capital expense questions. For corporation-specific tax filing, use T4012. For GST input tax credits, use RC4022. For fixed assets in this app, consider whether the cost should be expensed, capitalized, or reviewed by a CPA.
